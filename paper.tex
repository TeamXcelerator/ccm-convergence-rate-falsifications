\begin{abstract}
I revisit the three experiments in my earlier paper on the
Connes--Consani--Moscovici (CCM) construction: a Weil--prolate comparison,
a test motivated by \'Sliwi\'nski's spectral-error conjecture, and
auxiliary Mellin-transform comparisons. Corrected calculations and a
review of the quantities being compared change the conclusions.
The Weil--prolate experiment shows close finite sampled agreement and
does not test CCM Lemma~7.2. The spectral experiment now examines
maximum error along the prescribed scaling $\lambda=N$. Independent
interval checks account for all 1700 positive roots in seven saved
finite spectra. Across a tenfold increase in the parameter, maximum
error grows about 49-fold; growth persists after excluding the highest
20\% of roots. A coefficient-based trace bound independently excludes
small maximum errors in these saved spectra. The same calculations give increasingly accurate initial
roots. This separation between initial-root accuracy and expanding-window
error is evidence against the proposed uniform inverse-logarithmic decay
in the tested range. Analytic source-error control and an asymptotic
disproof remain open. The Mellin scans located real-part crossings
that fail the complex-zero check. A separate boundary argument proves
that the tested fixed-cutoff surrogates have only finitely many
critical-line zeros, although the untruncated weight represents zeta
exactly. This identifies a truncation obstruction without challenging
CCM's own transform. I withdraw the earlier falsification claims and
retain the corrected measurements, their limitations, and the
reproduction scripts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose of this correction}

The earlier version of this paper interpreted three numerical tests
as evidence against proposed explanations of the accuracy of the CCM
construction~\cite{CCM2025}. This revision addresses those same tests.
It corrects the attribution, numerical results, and conclusions.
Its stance on the spectral-error conjecture is empirical: the seven
finite comparisons give an adverse trend in the specified maximum-error
observable. This is evidence against the proposed decay in the tested
range, not a proof about the limit.

I apologize to the CCM authors for mislabeling the Weil--prolate
comparison as a test of their Lemma~7.2. That lemma compares suitably
normalized prolate and Hermite functions. The approximation of a
Weil state by a prolate trial is a different question. I withdraw
the claim that my experiment falsifies Lemma~7.2.

Two further corrections are necessary. \'Sliwi\'nski's
Conjecture~4.1 concerns maximum error, whereas the earlier rate
argument used mean error~\cite{Sliwinski2026}. The auxiliary Mellin
experiments located zeros of a real part, not validated complex zeros.
Neither comparison supports the earlier conclusions in the form
stated. The following sections report what the retained evidence
does support.

\section{Numerical conventions}

Write $C=\lambda^2$ and let $N$ be the positive Fourier cutoff.
The finite Weil matrix has dimension $2N+1$; its even sector has
dimension $N+1$. The experiments use a retained even coefficient
vector, the associated finite Weil function, and, where requested,
the positive movable roots of its secular expression. These finite
objects are not identified here with the limiting CCM state. Below I
identify the complete finite root list with the saved finite rank-one
spectrum; the full infinite spectrum is a different object.

The revised runs use arbitrary-precision arithmetic through the
Xcelerator Toolkit, with source identities, numerical settings, and
software revisions recorded in each journal. The letter $P$ denotes
requested decimal working precision, not certified output digits.
The $P=1000$ and $P=2000$ sources below use 3386 and 6708 bits,
respectively; numerical kernels may increase precision according to
their error checks. Historical outputs retain their original settings.

The current root-acquisition route is independent of the reference
zeta ordinates; the subsequent comparisons use a retained reference
dataset. Successful execution, finite hypothesis outcomes, and
supplemental diagnostic coverage are recorded separately. A completed
experiment can have a negative result or leave an asymptotic question
unanswered.

\section{Test 1: the Weil--prolate comparison}
\label{sec:prolate}

The trial is constructed from a zero-integral combination of the
even prolate modes with zero and four nodes, followed by the finite
sum map
\[
 h_\lambda=h_{4,\lambda}-r_\lambda h_{0,\lambda},\qquad
 r_\lambda=\frac{\int h_{4,\lambda}(x)\,dx}{\int h_{0,\lambda}(x)\,dx},
 \qquad k_\lambda(u)=\sqrt u\sum_{1\leq n\leq\lambda/u}h_\lambda(nu).
\]
The numerical implementation uses a three-point finite-difference
prolate model with zero endpoint values and piecewise-linear
interpolation. It is a finite-Dirichlet approximation, not a
certified evaluation of the regular continuum prolate functions.

For the $1024$ logarithmically spaced retained samples, let $x_i$
denote the finite Weil function and $g_i$ the trial. The reported
observable is the sampled maximum residual after least-squares scaling:
\[
 a_{\rm LS}=\frac{\sum_i x_i g_i}{\sum_i g_i^2},\qquad
 E_{\rm LS}=\frac{\max_i|x_i-a_{\rm LS}g_i|}{\max_i|x_i|}.
\]
It is neither a continuous supremum nor the minimax fit over scalars.

The current fits have been independently replayed on the serialized
sample values. At $C=1000$, the residual is approximately $0.7081\%$,
replacing the earlier $99.88\%$. The description of the profiles as
essentially uncorrelated is therefore withdrawn. The change cannot
be attributed to precision alone. At $C=100$, the two grid sizes
share the same Weil-source identities; grid refinement lowers the
residual by about $3.62\%$. Two grids do not establish the continuum
error or remove the need to control the finite-difference model.

CCM Lemma~7.2 gives an absolute uniform prolate--Hermite estimate,
after normalization. It is not a relative Weil--prolate error bound.
Moreover, finitely many values of $C E_{\rm LS}$, with changing
Fourier cutoff and numerical resolution, cannot disprove an
asymptotic bound with unspecified constant and onset. The defensible
result is close sampled agreement for these four finite configurations;
the asserted falsification does not survive.

The missing transfer estimate can be made explicit. Denote the preceding
sum map by $T_\lambda$. On $\lambda^{-1}\leq u\leq\lambda$, the triangle
inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 |T_\lambda h(u)|\leq\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt u}\|h\|_\infty,
 \qquad \|T_\lambda h\|_\infty\leq\lambda^{3/2}\|h\|_\infty.
 \label{eq:prolate-transfer}
\end{equation}
Even an absolute input error $O(\lambda^{-2})$ therefore yields only
the crude transferred bound $O(\lambda^{-1/2})$ by this argument.
This is an upper bound, not a prediction of the actual decay; cancellation
could improve it. A relative Weil--prolate estimate additionally needs
control of the Weil approximation and its normalization. Dunster's
uniform prolate asymptotics provide error bounds in specified parameter
regimes~\cite{Dunster2017}; applying such results here would still require
matching those regimes and controlling the finite-Dirichlet discretization.
This identifies what a genuine convergence test would have to measure.

\section{Test 2: the spectral-error comparison}
\label{sec:roots}

Let $\mu_1<\cdots<\mu_N$ be the complete positive movable-root list
of a retained finite source and let $\gamma_k$ be the $k$th positive
reference zeta ordinate. The experiment records both
\[
 A_N=\frac1N\sum_{k=1}^N|\mu_k-\gamma_k|,
 \qquad B_N=\max_{1\leq k\leq N}|\mu_k-\gamma_k|.
\]
It also retains a separately labeled trimmed mean. These are different
observables and are not interchanged here.

Dominik \'Sliwi\'nski clarified in correspondence that the intended
observable is maximum ($L^\infty$) error, rather than mean error.
His clarification prompted me to re-evaluate this test using maximum error.

His published Definitions~2.5--2.6 distinguish mean error $\epsilon$ from maximum error
$\mathcal E$. Conjecture~4.1 asserts the
existence of $\lim_{\kappa\to\infty}\mathcal E(\kappa)\log\kappa$
along $\lambda=N=\kappa$. A nonzero limit is suggested separately
after the displayed conjecture. The earlier paper substituted the
mean and treated the nonzero value as part of the conjecture.
Those statements are corrected here.

Crucially, Definition~2.6 maximizes over the $N$ positive eigenvalues
of the finite $(2N+1)$-dimensional operator in Definition~2.4.
For a saved even vector $w$, put $S=\sum_j w_j\ne0$, let
$D=\operatorname{diag}(d_j)$ be the Fourier diagonal, and set
$A=D-(Dw)\mathbf1^T/S$. The rank-one determinant identity gives
\[
 \det(zI-A)=\frac{z}{S}Q(z),\qquad
 Q(z)=\prod_{j=-N}^N(z-d_j)\sum_{j=-N}^N\frac{w_j}{z-d_j}.
\]
This is the finite construction underlying CCM Lemma~5.4.
For these saved sources all weights are nonzero. Independent directed
interval checks bracket $N$ distinct positive roots away from the poles;
symmetry supplies $N$ negative partners, exhausting the degree $2N$ of
$Q$. Thus the checked roots are the complete positive spectrum of the
saved finite matrix $A$. The unchanged lattice modes of the infinite
CCM operator do not belong in this finite comparison. A full-infinite-spectrum
indexing caveat would address a different observable.

The seven scripts 2a--2g have been run at
$\kappa=50,100,150,200,300,400,500$, with $C=\kappa^2$.
The completed runs use $P=1000$ through $\kappa=300$, $P=2000$
at $\kappa=400$, and $P=1500$ at $\kappa=500$.
Required measurements and applicable supplemental acquisitions are
accounted for through original runs and retained-source recoveries.
Table~\ref{tab:kappa} reports all seven current maximum-error rows.
The independent Rust verifier uses directed MPFR intervals and no
Toolkit numerical code. It checks all 1700 roots and computes errors
against the pinned reference dataset. Original journals support the
first five rows; authenticated published source/root artifacts support
the last two. The brackets certify the exact saved sources, not their
distance from the analytically defined Weil ground states.

$2.48\times10^{-69}$. These computations separate improving low-lying
accuracy from worsening error over an expanding window.

\subsection*{A check independent of root refinement}

The size of the full-window discrepancy can also be bounded from the
coefficients alone. Expanding the square of the finite matrix $A$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(A^2)=\sum_j d_j^2
 -\frac{2\sum_j w_jd_j^2}{S}
 +\left(\frac{\sum_jw_jd_j}{S}\right)^2.
\]
Even weights and antisymmetric poles make the last term zero. The
complete spectrum $0,\pm\mu_1,\ldots,\pm\mu_N$ therefore satisfies
\begin{equation}
 M_2:=\sum_{k=1}^N\mu_k^2
 =\frac12\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
 =\sum_{j=1}^N d_j^2-\frac{\sum_{j=-N}^N w_jd_j^2}{S}.
 \label{eq:spectral-moment}
\end{equation}
This trace identity does not require the displayed coordinate matrix
to be normal. The reverse triangle inequality for the Euclidean norm,
together with $\|\mu-\gamma\|_2\leq\sqrt N B_N$, yields
\begin{equation}
 B_N\geq\max\left\{0,
 \frac{\sqrt{M_2}-\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^N\gamma_k^2}}{\sqrt N}\right\}.
 \label{eq:moment-error}
\end{equation}

I evaluated these bounds locally with independent directed MPFR
intervals, using the exact saved coefficients and the pinned reference
ordinates rounded to each source precision. No root values enter

intervals overlaps the coefficient enclosure in all seven rows.
Thus the large discrepancies are not explained by root refinement
alone. This check still depends on the saved ground-state coefficients:
it does not certify their error relative to the analytic construction.
In particular, the weights are signed, so the ratio in
\eqref{eq:spectral-moment} is not a positive weighted average of $d_j^2$.
Large outlying roots cannot be rejected by an interlacing argument that
assumes positive weights.

\subsection*{Interpretation and remaining scope}

This is substantive evidence against inverse-logarithmic maximum-error
decay in the tested range, including the suggested faster powers of
the logarithm: the observed maximum increases rather than decreases.
It does not establish the eventual behavior. The remaining numerical
obligation is to propagate matrix-assembly and ground-state uncertainty
to the spectral error, supported by independent precision refinements.
An asymptotic disproof additionally needs an eventual lower bound or
another analytic contradiction. The new maximum-error table replaces
the earlier mean-based rate argument; it does not restore that argument.

There is also a window issue in the published supporting evidence:
Section~4 of~\cite{Sliwinski2026} reports comparisons with the first
1000 zeta zeros while extending $N=\kappa$ to 7500. For $N>1000$,
that reported reference window alone cannot establish the maximum
over all $N$ eigenvalues in Definition~2.6. A fixed window and an
expanding all-$N$ window require separate evidence.

Recent work also illustrates the strength of the initial-root phenomenon.
Groskin reports 307--329 matching digits for the first ten zeros in a
truncated-Weil calculation at his cutoff $c=100$, $N=250$~\cite{Groskin2026}.
That is a different finite configuration, and I do not independently
validate those numbers here. Such initial-window accuracy is compatible
with the separation observed above; it does not measure the all-$N$
maximum along $\lambda=N$.

$\lambda=N$, now have independently checked complete movable-root
lists and error arithmetic:

\section{Test 3: the auxiliary Mellin transforms}
\label{sec:transforms}

The two auxiliary transforms tested were
\[
 \omega(u)=\frac{u e^u}{(1+e^u)^2},\qquad
 F_\lambda(s)=\int_{\lambda^{-1}}^\lambda\omega(u)u^{s-1}\,du,
 \qquad
 G_{C,N}(s)=\int_{\lambda^{-1}}^\lambda f_{C,N}(u)\omega(u)u^{s-1}\,du,
\]
where $f_{C,N}$ is the finite Weil function. The scripts call these
the naive and weighted transforms. They scan the real part on
$s=1/2+it$, for $5\leq t\leq55$, using 5000 intervals and 30
requested refinement digits. The retained evaluations use $P=1000$.

A real-part crossing is not sufficient for a complex zero. The
revised checks evaluate both components at quadrature orders 500
and 1000, normalized by the corresponding absolute-weight integral,
with residual threshold $10^{-20}$.

\subsection*{A fixed-cutoff obstruction}

The untruncated weight is a classical zeta representation~\cite{DLMF}:
\begin{equation}
 F_\infty(s)=\int_0^\infty u^s\frac{e^u}{(1+e^u)^2}\,du
 =\Gamma(s+1)(1-2^{1-s})\zeta(s),\qquad \Re s>0.
 \label{eq:full-mellin}
\end{equation}
The apparent singularity at $s=1$ is removable. On $\Re s=1/2$ both prefactors
are nonzero, so this integral has exactly the zeta zeros on that line.
Hard truncation changes that conclusion.

\noindent\textbf{Proposition (fixed-cutoff obstruction).}
At $C=13$ and $C=100$, $F_\lambda$ has no critical-line zeros for
$|t|>100$ and $|t|>2000$, respectively. For either fixed cutoff,
the naive and nonzero finite cosine-weighted transforms have only
finitely many critical-line zeros.

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Set $L=\log\lambda$, $h(u)=e^u/(1+e^u)^2$, and
$q(x)=e^{3x/2}h(e^x)$. The naive transform is
$\int_{-L}^L q(x)e^{itx}\,dx$. Two integrations by parts give
\begin{equation}
 |F_\lambda(1/2+it)|\geq\frac{\delta}{|t|}-\frac{M}{t^2},\quad
 \delta=|q(L)-q(-L)|,\quad
 M=|q'(L)|+|q'(-L)|+\int_{-L}^L|q''(x)|\,dx.
 \label{eq:mellin-boundary}
\end{equation}
Writing $u=e^x$ and $r=\tanh(u/2)$ gives
$q'=q(3/2-ur)$ and
$q''=q((3/2-ur)^2-ur-u^2(1-r^2)/2)$.
Since $0<r<1$ and $h(u)\leq e^{-u}$,
\[
 M\leq B_\lambda:=
 q(-L)(3/2+\lambda^{-1})+q(L)(3/2+\lambda)
 +\frac{111\sqrt{\pi}}{16}.
\]
The last term integrates the upper bound
$u^{1/2}e^{-u}(9/4+4u+3u^2/2)$ over $(0,\infty)$.
Outward rounding at both 256 and 512 bits gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 C & \text{enclosure for }\delta & B_\lambda/\delta\text{ upper bound} \\
 \hline
 13 & [0.14049,\,0.14050] & 95 \\
 100 & [0.006450,\,0.006451] & 1911
\end{array}
\]
so~\eqref{eq:mellin-boundary} proves the stated conservative thresholds.

For $p(x)=f_{C,N}(e^x)$, a nonzero finite cosine polynomial in
$j\pi x/L$, endpoint derivatives agree and odd ones vanish. Analyticity
gives a first nonzero endpoint derivative of finite even order $k$.
The lower endpoint derivatives of $pq$ vanish. Integrating $k+2$ times
therefore gives a leading modulus at least
$|p^{(k)}(L)|\delta/|t|^{k+1}$ and remainder $O(|t|^{-k-2})$.
Both transforms are eventually nonzero, entire, and not identically
zero; only finitely many critical-line zeros remain.\hfill$\square$

These fixed surrogates cannot reproduce the unbounded sequence of
zeta critical-line zeros. This does not exclude off-line zeros,
certify the entire scanned interval, quantify a weighted threshold,
or address $\lambda$ or $N$ growing with height.

\subsection*{What increasing the cutoff does recover}

The obstruction is compatible with convergence of the transform values.
For every $\lambda>1$ and real $t$, the omitted tails obey
\begin{equation}
 \left|F_\infty(1/2+it)-F_\lambda(1/2+it)\right|
 \leq \frac{1}{6\lambda^{3/2}}
 +\sqrt\lambda\,e^{-\lambda}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\right).
 \label{eq:mellin-tails}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $h(u)\leq1/4$ bounds the lower tail by
$\int_0^{1/\lambda}u^{1/2}/4\,du=1/(6\lambda^{3/2})$.
For the upper tail, use $h(u)\leq e^{-u}$ and the concavity bound
$\sqrt{\lambda+v}\leq\sqrt\lambda+v/(2\sqrt\lambda)$, then integrate
against $e^{-v}$ for $v\geq0$. Neither estimate depends on $t$.
For example, the right-hand side is less than $0.005422$ at
$\lambda=10$, and less than $0.000167$ at $\lambda=100$.

At any critical-line zeta zero, \eqref{eq:full-mellin} makes the full
integral zero, so \eqref{eq:mellin-tails} bounds the truncated integral's
residual there. A small residual does not force an exact zero on the
same line. Hence two facts coexist: increasing $\lambda$ recovers the
full transform uniformly in absolute value on that line, while fixing
either tested cutoff eventually excludes critical-line zeros.
The bound gives a sufficient accuracy budget for transform values;
it does not control zero locations. Such control would require, for
example, a contour estimate comparing the tail with the full transform
away from its zeros.

CCM Proposition~5.9 and Theorem~5.10 use a different multiplicative
Fourier transform representing the secular spectral information under
their hypotheses. Claim~3a's same-source Fourier/secular control passed.
Neither this obstruction nor the crossing tests support the earlier
claim that CCM accuracy cannot be explained by an integral transform.

\section{Conclusion}

The revised results show close finite Weil--prolate agreement and a
definite obstruction for the tested fixed Mellin cutoffs, whose
untruncated counterpart represents zeta exactly.
All seven checked finite spectra give an adverse trend in the specified
expanding-window maximum, even after excluding the highest 20\% of roots.
The coefficient-based moment bounds independently rule out small
full-window errors for those same saved sources.
The proposed uniform decay is empirically unsupported in this range.
Independent analytic source control and an eventual lower bound are
the next steps toward a stronger conclusion.
